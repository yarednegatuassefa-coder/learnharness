# Investor Intelligence Research Library

A curated external-source catalogue for optional study in the Strategic Learning Programme. It is a research index, not an investment signal service, paid-data replacement, or recommendation engine. Nothing here is a mandatory assignment.

## What is included

- 50 submitted entries with canonical links where the URL was sufficiently clear.
- Topic classification, editorial source-quality tier, suggested learning priority, and a practical caution for each source.
- A searchable in-app catalogue with filters for topic, quality tier and priority.
- Five optional learning pathways and a 12-week sequence.

The source metadata is an initial editorial assessment, not a formal audit of every site's uptime, data licence, accuracy or current free-tier limits. Recheck terms, publication dates, access restrictions and original-source provenance before relying on a source.

## Source-quality tiers

- **A — Primary / institutional / professional:** regulators, central banks, official company disclosures, academic or professional research. Prefer these for facts and source-of-record evidence.
- **B — Specialist data / analytical publisher:** useful aggregators, research publishers and market tools. Use for discovery and analysis, then verify important claims against primary data.
- **C — Commentary / community / selected theses:** useful for hypotheses, frameworks and debate, but not authoritative evidence by itself.

Priority is about learning sequence, not investment merit:
- **P1 — Core:** essential foundations for reading filings, valuing businesses and understanding macroeconomic conditions.
- **P2 — Applied:** useful once the foundations are in place.
- **P3 — Supplementary:** optional perspectives, idea discovery and specialist tools.

## Five research pathways

### 1. Read the source of truth
Start with SEC EDGAR, Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letters, Federal Reserve, ECB, BIS, FRED and Investor.gov. Learn to identify the document type, reporting period, accounting basis, footnotes, revisions and publication date. Aggregators are navigation aids; primary filings and datasets are the evidentiary record.

### 2. Understand a business before valuing its shares
Use annual reports and SEC filings to understand the business model, revenue drivers, margins, working capital, debt, dilution, cash conversion, competitive position and risks. Then use Damodaran's datasets and valuation material to build explicit assumptions. Reconcile summary platforms against audited statements.

### 3. Understand the macro regime
Use FRED, Federal Reserve, ECB and BIS for rates, inflation, credit, liquidity and financial-system data. Use specialist aggregators to discover series, then verify definitions and vintages. Build competing scenarios rather than treating one forecast as certain.

### 4. Understand portfolios, risk and implementation
Study AQR, CFA Institute, Bogleheads, Morningstar, ETF Database, Portfolio Visualizer and Curvo. Learn diversification, factor exposure, fees, tracking error, drawdowns, taxes, currency risk, liquidity and benchmark selection. Treat backtests as conditional historical experiments, not forecasts.

### 5. Understand investor behaviour and competing theses
Read Oaktree memos, Morgan Housel, Of Dollars and Data, A Wealth of Common Sense and selected investment theses. Extract the claim, evidence, incentives, time horizon, disconfirming evidence and what would change the conclusion. Do not copy trades or conclusions without independent analysis.

## Suggested 12-week sequence

| Week | Focus | Core sources | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Financial statements and filings | SEC EDGAR, Berkshire letters, AnnualReports | One-page company business and filing map |
| 2 | Accounting quality and cash flow | SEC EDGAR, Damodaran, Stock Analysis | Reconcile reported earnings to cash flow |
| 3 | Business quality and competitive position | Berkshire letters, Oaktree memos, annual reports | Business-quality memo with risks |
| 4 | Valuation foundations | Damodaran data and blog, CFI | Simple valuation model with explicit assumptions |
| 5 | Interest rates and inflation | FRED, Federal Reserve, ECB | Macro dashboard with source definitions |
| 6 | Credit and financial cycles | BIS, Bridgewater, Oaktree | Credit-cycle brief with competing scenarios |
| 7 | Portfolio theory and diversification | AQR, CFA Institute, Bogleheads | Portfolio exposure and risk map |
| 8 | Funds, fees and implementation | ETF Database, Morningstar, Curvo | ETF comparison using current issuer factsheets |
| 9 | Backtesting and model risk | Portfolio Visualizer, AQR | Backtest critique covering survivorship, fees and regime dependence |
| 10 | Institutional disclosures | SEC 13F filings, Dataroma, WhaleWisdom | Dated holdings comparison—not a copy-trade list |
| 11 | Investor psychology | Collaborative Fund, Of Dollars and Data, A Wealth of Common Sense | Decision journal and behavioural error analysis |
| 12 | Independent investment thesis | Primary filings + Damodaran + macro sources | Thesis, valuation range, risks, disconfirming evidence and monitoring triggers |

Use this as a flexible sequence. Spend more time where your diagnostic reveals weak understanding. The final thesis is an educational exercise, not a recommendation to buy or sell.

## Critical interpretation rules

1. **13F filings are lagged.** They are generally filed after quarter-end, show only reportable long positions for covered managers, and omit important exposures such as most shorts, cash, derivatives and non-reportable assets. A 13F is not a real-time portfolio or a complete view of a manager's strategy.
2. **Insider and politician disclosures are not automatic signals.** Transactions can be planned, routine, diversified, delayed in disclosure or driven by liquidity/tax needs. Disclosure does not prove informational advantage or predictive value.
3. **Aggregators are not the source of record.** Confirm material figures, transactions and claims in the original filing, issuer document or statistical release.
4. **Backtests are fragile.** Check look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, selection effects, data revisions, trading costs, taxes, liquidity, currency, benchmark choice and out-of-sample performance.
5. **Valuation is conditional.** A model is a set of assumptions, not an objective price oracle. Show sensitivity to growth, margins, reinvestment, discount rates and terminal value.
6. **Research has incentives.** Asset managers, brokers, venture funds, data vendors and commentators have institutional, commercial or reputational perspectives. Identify who benefits if the reader accepts the thesis.
7. **Free access can change.** Some sites have paywalls, rate limits, account requirements, regional restrictions or commercial data terms. Do not scrape or redistribute content in violation of those terms.

## How to use it in LearnHarness

Open **Strategic Learning Programme → Investor Intelligence Library**. Search by source, topic or use case; filter by source tier and priority; open the source in a browser; then start a normal LearnHarness session using the relevant pathway. Bring back exact source titles, publication dates, filing periods, page numbers or dataset series IDs. LearnHarness should distinguish primary evidence, third-party interpretation and its own inference.

Suggested session goal:

> Diagnose my understanding of financial statements, business quality, valuation and macroeconomic risk. Use the Investor Intelligence Library as a source map, prioritise primary evidence, and build a staged path from filings to an independent investment thesis. For every material claim, identify the source, date, assumptions, incentives, counter-evidence and uncertainty. Do not recommend a trade or treat disclosed holdings as signals.
