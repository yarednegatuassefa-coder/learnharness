# Prioritized 12-month optional reading sequence

This is a suggested path, not a deadline or assignment queue. Two core books per month; choose one if workload or priorities require it. Finish with an applied artifact and a delayed retrieval session rather than a completion badge.

| Month | Core reading | Discipline links | Applied output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Thinking in Systems — Donella Meadows; Superforecasting — Philip E. Tetlock and Dan Gardner | Philosophy, Psychology, Business, Healthcare Data Analysis | Decision log with probability estimates, outcomes and calibration review. |
| 2 | The Machiavellians — James Burnham; Seeing Like a State — James C. Scott | Political Science, History, Philosophy | Map formal authority, informal elites, incentives and information failures in an institution. |
| 3 | Chip War — Chris Miller; Apple in China — Patrick McGee | Geopolitics, Supply Chain, Business | Semiconductor value-chain map identifying chokepoints and strategic dependencies. |
| 4 | 7 Powers — Hamilton Helmer; Understanding Michael Porter — Joan Magretta | Business, Economics | Competitive-advantage audit of one operating business. |
| 5 | The Power Broker — Robert A. Caro; SPQR — Mary Beard | Political Science, History | Compare bureaucratic infrastructure power with Roman political institutions. |
| 6 | Algorithms to Live By — Brian Christian and Tom Griffiths; A Man for All Markets — Edward O. Thorp | Economics, Psychology, Healthcare Data Analysis | Decision memo using base rates, expected value and uncertainty ranges. |
| 7 | Designing Data-Intensive Applications — Martin Kleppmann; Working in Public — Nadia Eghbal | Business, AI/model auditing | Audit a software system's reliability assumptions and map maintainer incentives. |
| 8 | The Idea Factory — Jon Gertner; The Beginning of Infinity — David Deutsch | Philosophy, History, Business | Innovation-system case study: institutions, incentives and error correction. |
| 9 | Boyd — Robert Coram; Postwar — Tony Judt | Geopolitics, Political Science, History | Compare military adaptation with post-conflict political reconstruction. |
| 10 | Status and Culture — W. David Marx; Antimemetics — Nadia Asparouhova | Psychology, Strategic Communication | Analyse a public narrative through status, attention and diffusion. |
| 11 | Not According to Plan — Maria Belodubrovskaya; The Nolan Variations — Tom Shone | Film, Strategic Communication, History | Film-analysis dossier on visual language, institutions and audience framing. |
| 12 | Sea People — Christina Thompson; Project Hail Mary — Andy Weir | History, Geopolitics, Systems thinking | Capstone comparing navigation under uncertainty with fictional engineering problem-solving. |

## Optional substitutions
- Health: Exercised — Daniel E. Lieberman; Outlive — Peter Attia with Bill Gifford. Verify health claims against current clinical evidence.
- Business execution: Unreasonable Hospitality — Will Guidara; The Hard Thing About Hard Things — Ben Horowitz.
- Computing history: Alan Turing: The Enigma — Andrew Hodges; Where Wizards Stay Up Late — Katie Hafner and Matthew Lyon.
- State formation: Against the Grain — James C. Scott; The Ancient City — Numa Denis Fustel de Coulanges.
- Science and ethics through fiction: Remembrance of Earth’s Past — Cixin Liu; The Invincible — Stanisław Lem.

## Monthly learning protocol
1. Choose one core title based on the current learning goal; the second is optional.
2. Write 3–5 questions before reading.
3. Capture claims with page references; separate author claims, evidence, interpretation and critique.
4. Produce the applied output. Finishing a book is not proof of mastery.
5. Run a LearnHarness retrieval session 7–14 days later and test transfer to a different case.
6. Update reading status and notes. Do not auto-create mandatory lessons or progress badges.
